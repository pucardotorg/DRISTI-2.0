# Domain actors & roles

Snapshot: 2026-07-30. Sourced from the §138 relevance profile on the
[domain model site](https://dristidomain.netlify.app/)
(`national_institutions`, party terms).

**These are domain actors, not product personas.** This file does not claim
who logs into DRISTI. See [../open-questions.md](../open-questions.md).

## Parties & representation

| Actor | Domain meaning |
|---|---|
| **Payee** | The person named in the instrument as the one to whom payment is to be made. |
| **Holder in due course** | A holder who took the instrument for value, in good faith and before maturity. |
| **Complainant** | The payee or holder in due course of the dishonoured cheque - the only person who can set a s.138 case going, by filing the complaint. |
| **Drawer** | The person who makes (draws) the cheque; the accused in a s.138 case. |
| **Accused** | The drawer of the cheque, prosecuted for the dishonour; where the drawer is a company, the company and every person in charge of and responsible to it. |
| **Person in charge** | The director or officer who, at the time of the offence, was in charge of and responsible to the company for its business - the test for vicarious liability. |
| **Company liability** | Where the drawer is a company, the persons in charge of and responsible to it for the conduct of its business are also liable. |
| **Drawee** | The banker on whom the cheque is drawn and who is directed to pay. |
| **Banker** | A person carrying on the business of banking; the drawee of a cheque. |
| **Advocate** | The enrolled legal practitioner who appears for the complainant or the accused; the right to appear flows from the Advocates Act. |
| **Holder** | The person entitled in their own name to possession of the instrument and to receive its amount. |

## Judiciary

The court hierarchy a s.138 case moves through: the trial court is the Judicial Magistrate of the First Class; appeal lies to the Court of Session; revision and quashing to the High Court; and, by special leave, to the Supreme Court. Alongside the judges sits the court's ministerial establishment.

### Court tiers (s.138 path)

| Tier | Role in s.138 | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **Supreme Court of India** | Final appeal, by special leave | The highest court. A s.138 matter reaches it only by special leave (art.136); its law binds every court in India (art.141). |
| **High Court of the State** | Revision, quashing, writ, superintendence | The State's highest court. In a s.138 case it hears criminal revisions and petitions to quash the complaint (s.482 CrPC / s.528 BNSS) and superintends the subordinate courts. |
| **Court of Session (District & Sessions Court)** | First appeal from conviction | Headed by the District & Sessions Judge. It hears the first appeal from a s.138 conviction. |
| **Chief Judicial Magistrate** | Administrative head of the magistracy | Heads the magistracy of a district and has administrative control over the Judicial Magistrates. |
| **Judicial Magistrate of the First Class** | The s.138 trial court | The trial court for a s.138 case: it takes cognizance of the complaint, tries it summarily, and on conviction sentences the accused. |

### Court roles (judges & ministerial staff)

| Role | Category | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **District & Sessions Judge** | judge | Heads the district judiciary; hears sessions trials and s.138 appeals. |
| **Judicial Magistrate (First Class)** | judge | Tries the s.138 complaint - takes cognizance, records evidence, delivers judgment and sentence. |
| **Sheristadar / Chief Ministerial Officer** | staff | Head of the court's ministerial establishment; signs and issues the summons 'by order of the Court'. |
| **Bench Clerk** | staff | Maintains the court's daily records, exhibits and order sheet during hearings. |
| **Registry / Scrutiny officer** | staff | The court office that receives and scrutinises the filed complaint for defects before it goes for cognizance. |
| **Registrar (High Court)** | staff | Heads the High Court registry - scrutiny, listing, custody of records. |
| **Process server / Summons bailiff** | staff | Serves the summons and warrants the court issues. |
| **Stenographer / Interpreter** | staff | Records dictation and interprets a deposition into the language the State has notified as the court language. |

## Police

The police hierarchy. The Police Act, 1861 constitutes the force under an Inspector-General with a Superintendent in each district; the modern rank ladder (Constable up to Director General) and the units are fixed by each State's Police Act. Senior ranks are Indian Police Service (IPS) officers recruited through the UPSC Civil Services Examination; the rest are the State Police Service.

In a s.138 case the police role is primarily **process execution** (summons /
warrants), not FIR-led investigation. Full rank ladder and units are in the profile;
abbreviated here.

### Ranks (high → low)

- **Director General of Police & State Police Chief** — The administration, supervision, direction and control of the entire State police, subject to the Government.
- **Director General of Police** — Apex rank; heads statewide wings and branches (crime, intelligence, armed police, etc.).
- **Additional Director General of Police** — Heads a zone or a major functional wing.
- **Inspector General of Police** — Heads a zone or range or wing. The 1861 Act's Inspector-General was the historical head of the force.
- **Deputy Inspector General of Police** — Heads a police range - a group of districts.
- **Superintendent of Police** — District Police Chief - commands the whole police of a district. Registers and supervises serious cases.
- **Deputy Superintendent of Police** — Sub-divisional officer; supervises a group of circles and stations.
- **Inspector of Police** — Heads a circle; Station House Officer of larger stations; supervises the Sub-Inspectors under him.
- **Sub-Inspector of Police** — Station House Officer of most police stations - registers the FIR, investigates, files the charge-sheet.
- **Assistant Sub-Inspector of Police** — Assists the SHO; may hold charge of an outpost.
- **Police Head Constable** — Senior constable; supervises constables and beat duty.
- **Police Constable** — The basic rank - patrol, guard, beat and first response.

### Units

- **State Police Force** (head: State Police Chief / DGP) — One unified State force, divided into wings, units and branches by the Government.
- **Zone** (head: ADGP / IGP) — A cluster of ranges.
- **Range** (head: DIG) — A group of districts.
- **District** (head: Superintendent of Police) — The basic territorial police command.
- **Commissionerate (metropolitan)** (head: Commissioner of Police) — Unitary metropolitan policing for the larger cities a State notifies.
- **Sub-division** (head: Dy.SP) — A group of circles within a district.
- **Circle** (head: Circle Inspector) — A group of police stations.
- **Police Station** (head: Station House Officer) — The primary unit; its jurisdiction is notified and it must be functional at all hours.
- **Outpost / Beat** (head: ASI / Head Constable) — The smallest sub-units of a station for local coverage.
