# National §138 case journey

Snapshot: 2026-07-30. Sourced from `national_process` in the §138 profile on the
[domain model site](https://dristidomain.netlify.app/).

The prescribed procedure for a Section 138 case under central law - the Negotiable Instruments Act read with the criminal procedure code (BNSS, earlier the CrPC). This is the same in every state until a state's own rules of practice add to it; the Prescribed lens shows the statutory deadlines. State-specific timings and rules are layered on top as each state is processed.

**Timing note:** Prescribed shows the statutory deadline where the NI Act or the code sets one; Regular court and ON Court timings are state-specific and are filled in as each state's rules and practice are processed.

This is the **prescribed national process**, not a product UX journey.

## 1 · Dishonour & statutory notice

*Prescribed timing:* Notice within 30 days of the dishonour memo

- The cheque, presented within its validity, is returned unpaid for insufficiency of funds or because it exceeds the arrangement; the bank issues a dishonour memo. _NI Act §138, NI Act §72_
- The payee sends a written demand notice to the drawer within 30 days of receiving the dishonour memo, demanding the cheque amount. _NI Act §138_

## 2 · The 15-day window to pay

*Prescribed timing:* 15 days for the drawer to pay

- The drawer has 15 days from receiving the notice to pay the amount. If it is paid, no offence arises. _NI Act §138_

## 3 · Cause of action & the complaint

*Prescribed timing:* Complaint within 1 month of the cause of action

- If the amount is unpaid within 15 days, the offence is complete and the cause of action arises. _NI Act §138, NI Act §142_
- The payee or holder in due course files a written complaint before a Judicial Magistrate of the First Class with jurisdiction (ordinarily where the payee's bank branch lies), within one month of the cause of action; delay may be condoned for sufficient cause. _NI Act §142, BNSS §514_

## 4 · Cognizance & examination of the complainant

- The Magistrate takes cognizance of the offence on the complaint; the complainant's evidence may be given on affidavit. _BNSS §210, BNSS §223, NI Act §145_
- The offence is tried only by a court not below a Judicial Magistrate of the First Class. _NI Act §142_

## 5 · Issue of process (summons) to the accused

- The Magistrate issues summons to the accused. A summons may be served by speed post or an approved courier and is deemed served even if the accused refuses it. _BNSS §227, BNSS §63, NI Act §144_

## 6 · Appearance, accusation & plea

- The accused appears; the substance of the accusation is stated and the plea recorded. A s.138 case is tried as a summary trial. _NI Act §143, BNSS §285_
- The court may direct the drawer to pay interim compensation of up to 20% of the cheque amount. _NI Act §143A_

## 7 · Evidence & presumptions

- The complainant's evidence is led, usually on affidavit, and the accused may cross-examine. The bank's dishonour slip is prima facie proof of dishonour, and the court presumes the cheque was issued for a debt or liability unless the accused rebuts it. _NI Act §145, NI Act §146, NI Act §139_

## 8 · Judgment

*Prescribed timing:* Endeavour to conclude within 6 months of filing

- After final arguments the Magistrate delivers judgment. The trial is conducted summarily and the court is to endeavour to conclude it within six months of the filing of the complaint. _NI Act §143_

## 9 · Sentence, compensation & compounding

- On conviction the drawer is liable to imprisonment up to two years, or a fine up to twice the cheque amount, or both, and the court ordinarily awards compensation to the payee. _NI Act §138, BNSS §395_
- The offence is compoundable at any stage, so the parties may settle and close the case. _NI Act §147, BNSS §359_

## 10 · Appeal

- An appeal against conviction lies to the Court of Session. Where compensation or a fine was awarded, the appellate court may require the appellant to deposit a minimum of 20% of it. _BNSS §415, NI Act §148_
